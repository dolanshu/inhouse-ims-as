# capacity-estimate (Capacity Order-of-Magnitude Study)

> **Status: non-committed values.** All numbers in this document are derived from public statistics, not measured.
> Per `AGENT.md` §2, before the M6 measurement no CPS or concurrency number may be published externally,
> nor may the numbers in this document be used as SLA or acceptance basis.
>
> Purpose: to provide a **magnitude** input for the SIP stack selection (ADR-0019), answering one question ——
> is capacity a differentiator for the selection, or not.

### 2.1 Two Corrections to Keep in Mind

**Correction one: SIM count is not the traffic base.**
Penetration of 441% means an average of 4.4 cards per person. Traffic is generated by people, not by cards.
Therefore all traffic estimates uniformly use **natural persons** (7.5–9.0 million), not subscriber count.

**Correction two: Hong Kong cellular voice is extremely heavily substituted by OTT.**
International traffic fell to about 1/10 of its peak in 15 years (7.76 billion → 733 million minutes/year).
WhatsApp / FaceTime carry most voice traffic. Therefore per-user cellular voice minutes (MOU)
should take a **low value**, not the typical MOU of mainland or European/US markets.

## 3. Concurrency and CPS Derivation Chain

| Step | Assumption | Value | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Traffic base (natural persons) | 7.5 million | — |
| 2 | Per-user monthly cellular voice minutes (MOU) | **40 min** (sensitive range 30–60) | 300 million minutes/month |
| 3 | Convert to daily traffic | ÷ 30 | 10 million minutes/day |
| 4 | Busy-hour share of the day | **16%** (mobile typical 15–18%) | 1.6 million minutes/busy-hour |
| 5 | **Network-wide busy-hour concurrency (Erlang)** | busy-hour minutes ÷ 60 | **≈ 26,700 Erlang** |
| 6 | Average call duration (ACD) | **40 s** (mobile voice typical 35–45 s) | — |
| 7 | **Network-wide busy-hour attempts BHCA** | Erlang × 3600 ÷ ACD | ≈ 2.4 million attempts/busy-hour |
| 8 | **Network-wide busy-hour CPS** | BHCA ÷ 3600 | ≈ **667 CPS** |
| 9 | **Single major MNO busy-hour** | ÷ 5 (uneven share, leader ~1/4) | **130–170 CPS / 5,300–6,700 Erlang** |

### 3.1 Sensitivity

MOU is the weakest link in the whole chain. The range:

| MOU | Network-wide Erlang | Network-wide CPS | Single-MNO CPS |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30 (OTT substitution more thorough) | 20,000 | 500 | 100–130 |
| **40 (adopted value)** | **26,700** | **667** | **130–170** |
| 60 (conservative / high enterprise-voice share) | 40,000 | 1,000 | 200–250 |

## 4. How Much the AS Will Actually Carry

The AS is not a full bypass —— it only processes the **subset** triggered by iFC (architecture §1, single ISC semantic, ADR-0003).
So the AS-side load depends on the business form, not the whole-network traffic:

| Trigger scenario | Share of network-wide busy-hour attempts | AS-side busy-hour CPS | AS-side concurrent dialogs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Anti-fraud screening: international / roaming / suspicious ranges only | very low (whole-network international only ~30 CPS) | **5–15 CPS** | 200–600 |
| Anti-fraud screening: network-wide called-side screening | 10–20% | **15–35 CPS** | 600–1,400 |
| Number translation / number change: specific trunk full volume | 30–50% | **40–85 CPS** | 1,600–3,400 |
| Extreme: all mobile-originating traffic through AS | 100% | **130–170 CPS** | 5,300–6,700 |

### 4.1 Design Target for Stack Selection (with growth margin)

Take the worst case (all traffic through AS) and leave a 3× margin:

| Target | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Busy-hour CPS | **≥ 500 CPS** | 170 × 3 ≈ 510 |
| Concurrent dialogs | **≥ 20,000** | 6,700 × 3 ≈ 20,100 |
| Scaling method | Multi-replica horizontal scaling | architecture §2/§4 decided: stateless signaling plane, state in Redis |

## 5. Conclusion for SIP Stack Selection

Against the nominal capabilities recorded in `docs/SIP_stack_selection.md`:

| Stack | Nominal capability | Against 500 CPS / 20,000 concurrent |
|---|---|---|
| sippy (Python) | 150–400 CPS, 5,000–10,000 concurrent (single process) | Needs 2–4 processes to cover the worst case; allowed under the multi-replica architecture anyway |
| go-b2bua (Go) | Same author's port, coroutine model | Has margin |
| reSIProcate (C++) | Carrier-grade (Sipwise NGCP, CounterPath in use) | Large margin |
| libre / baresip (C) | Industrial-grade, low memory footprint | Large margin |

### 5.1 Key conclusion: capacity is not a differentiator

**Under the "multi-replica + stateless" architecture, even sippy's nominal capability is sufficient.**
500 CPS split across 4 replicas is 125 CPS each, within sippy's nominal range.

Therefore a stack change **cannot be argued on capacity**: this capacity derivation is neither a reason to eliminate any candidate stack
nor a reason to keep any candidate stack. The stack choice should fall on other dimensions (maintainability, supply-chain risk,
homogeneity with the behavior baseline, build-chain cost).

### 5.2 The only valid use of capacity argument

The legitimate role of capacity data is as a **screening-out** item:
it rules out the claim "we must go C/C++ to be sufficient," thereby letting the stack choice be decided by maintainability,
homogeneity with the behavior baseline, and build-chain cost, rather than driven by performance fear.

## 6. Known Gaps in This Document

Recorded honestly for M6 measurement to reclaim:

| # | Gap | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | MOU=40 is an industry experience value, not a Hong Kong measurement. OFCA does not publish cellular-voice MOU | Whole chain ±50%, see §3.1 |
| C2 | Busy-hour share 16% does not distinguish weekday/weekend/holiday | Peak may be higher |
| C3 | MNO share assumed evenly split; actual leader operator is larger | Single-operator peak may reach 250 CPS |
| C4 | MVNO traffic (26 operators) not counted, nor which host network it lands on | Underestimates leading MNO |
| C5 | Fixed-network VoIP / enterprise PBX traffic not counted | Underestimates if AS also takes fixed-network triggers |
| C6 | All are busy-hour **hourly averages**, not the busiest 1-second peak within the busy hour | True peak CPS typically 1.5–2× |
| C7 | International traffic data only includes international, not local voice | No public data for local voice |

C6 is especially important: operator capacity planning usually looks at the 100-second peak or 1-second peak; this document only gives the hourly average.
M6 measurement must produce the peak distribution.
